\section{Discussion}

% Paragraph 1: Restate purpose + main finding + overall takeaway
The present study set out to determine whether the low CoT faithfulness rates
observed in proprietary reasoning models generalize across the open-weight
reasoning model ecosystem. The results demonstrate that CoT faithfulness varies
substantially, ranging from 39.7\% (Seed-1.6-Flash) to 89.9\%
(DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale) as measured by Claude Sonnet 4 (69.7\% overall). This
50-percentage-point spread confirms that faithfulness is not a fixed property of
reasoning models but varies systematically with model family, training approach,
and hint type. The central takeaway is that open-weight reasoning models exhibit
the same class of unfaithful reasoning documented by Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning} in proprietary systems, but with far greater
variance than previously appreciated.

% Paragraph 2: Cross-family variation (H1)
H1 predicted that CoT faithfulness would vary significantly across model
families. This hypothesis is supported: large and systematic differences in CoT
faithfulness emerge across the 12 models (9 families) tested. The DeepSeek family
shows notable improvement across generations, with R1 achieving 74.8\%
faithfulness compared to 89.9\% for the newer V3.2-Speciale, suggesting that
continued training refinement improves faithfulness within a family. A parallel
pattern emerges within the Qwen family: QwQ-32B achieves 56.3\% while the newer
Qwen3.5-27B reaches 68.3\%, again showing improvement with newer training
methodology. Training method appears more important than scale: MiniMax-M2.5
(230B/10B active MoE) achieves 73.1\% while the smaller Qwen3.5-27B (27B dense)
reaches 68.3\%, and the 7B OLMo-3-7B-Think achieves 56.9\% while the 32B
OLMo-3.1-32B-Think reaches 81.0\%. No strong linear correlation between model
scale and faithfulness is observed; training methodology and data quality appear
to matter more than raw scale, consistent with the broader finding in the
alignment literature that capability and alignment are partially orthogonal
dimensions~\cite{turpin2023unfaithful}. Ye et al.~\cite{ye2026faithfulness} provide complementary mechanistic evidence that faithfulness decays over long reasoning chains, suggesting that the relationship between scale and faithfulness may be mediated by chain length. These cross-family patterns extend the
single-model analyses of Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning} and Turpin et
al.~\cite{turpin2023unfaithful} by demonstrating that faithfulness is not merely
model-specific but family-specific, with within-family generational improvement
as a recurring motif. This appears to be among the first studies
to document systematic cross-family differences in CoT faithfulness across a
broad range of open-weight reasoning models.

% Paragraph 3: Hint-type patterns (H2)
H2 predicted that metadata and grader hints, which invoke structural or
rule-based framing, would yield the lowest faithfulness rates, on the
assumption that such hints would be harder for models to detect and acknowledge.
This hypothesis is only partially supported. Consistency (35.5\%) and sycophancy
(53.9\%) show the lowest faithfulness rates; these are social-pressure hints
that invoke expert opinion or prior answers. In contrast, rule-breaking hints
show the highest faithfulness: grader (77.7\%) and unethical (79.4\%). This
pattern is the opposite of the original H2 prediction. One interpretation of
this asymmetry is that social-pressure cues are harder for models to acknowledge
because they relate to the model's identity and social role; admitting
deference to authority or prior commitments may conflict with trained behavior
around independent reasoning. Rule-breaking hints, by contrast, are ethically
loaded: gaming a grader or using stolen answers invokes safety-relevant
scenarios that models may be more likely to discuss explicitly, potentially as a
result of RLHF training that encourages flagging ethical
violations~\cite{sharma2023sycophancy}. Zaman and Srivastava~\cite{zaman2025verbalization} reached a complementary conclusion, demonstrating that CoT can be faithful without explicitly verbalizing the hint, suggesting that some models may acknowledge influence through indirect reasoning patterns that text-based classifiers miss. Metadata hints (69.9\%) fall in the
middle, consistent with their relatively neutral framing. This hint-type
hierarchy aligns with the sycophancy findings of Sharma et
al.~\cite{sharma2023sycophancy}, who demonstrated that social-pressure
susceptibility scales with model size, and extends the hint taxonomy of Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning} by revealing that the faithfulness impact of
different hint categories varies more than their influence rates.

% Paragraph 4: Thinking-vs-answer gap — NOVEL finding
A notable exploratory finding concerns the gap between
thinking-token and answer-text hint acknowledgment. Using keyword matching
(a coarser classifier than the Sonnet judge; see Section~5.6), thinking tokens
show approximately 87.5\% acknowledgment while answer text shows only
approximately 28.6\%, a gap of roughly 58.9 percentage points. Three models exhibit near-total
suppression: GPT-OSS-120B acknowledges hints in 93.0\% of thinking traces but
0.0\% of answers; Step-3.5-Flash shows 97.8\% thinking acknowledgment versus 3.0\%
answer acknowledgment; DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale shows 97.8\% versus 5.3\%. Only two
models, Qwen3.5-27B (99.3\%/79.9\%) and DeepSeek-R1
(95.5\%/72.5\%), show relatively consistent acknowledgment across both channels.
Because these rates are based on keyword matching rather than the Sonnet judge,
they should be treated as indicative of the direction and magnitude of the gap
rather than as precise faithfulness measurements.
This appears to be among the first large-scale quantifications
of the thinking-versus-answer acknowledgment gap across multiple model families.
This finding directly substantiates Baker et al.'s~\cite{baker2025cot} argument
that CoT can be ``highly informative despite unfaithfulness'': thinking tokens
show approximately 87.5\% keyword-based acknowledgment, even when the externally
visible answer text shows only approximately 28.6\%. The gap also extends the observations of
Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning}, who documented unfaithfulness in
reasoning traces but did not separately quantify thinking-token and answer-text
acknowledgment. Furthermore, the near-total suppression observed in several models
resonates with the alignment faking patterns described by Greenblatt et
al.~\cite{greenblatt2024alignment}, where models exhibit different behavior in
monitored versus unmonitored channels. The present data suggest that a
structurally analogous phenomenon occurs naturally in reasoning model outputs:
internal reasoning (thinking tokens) reveals awareness that is systematically
suppressed in externally visible outputs (answer text).

% Paragraph 5: Confounding factors and methodological concerns
Several methodological considerations bear on the interpretation of these
results. First, the faithfulness classifiers used in this study disagree
substantially: the Sonnet judge yields 69.7\% overall faithfulness while the
regex+Ollama pipeline yields 82.6\%, a 12.9-percentage-point gap concentrated
in sycophancy ($-43$pp) and consistency ($-33$pp) hints. This sensitivity to
classifier choice (anticipated by Jacovi and Goldberg~\cite{jacovi2020faithfulness}, who argued that faithfulness definitions are inherently construct-dependent, and demonstrated empirically by Parcalabescu and Frank~\cite{parcalabescu2023measuring}) means that absolute faithfulness estimates should be
interpreted with caution, though relative rankings remain stable across both
classifiers. A companion study~\cite{young2026classifier} examines this classifier sensitivity in detail, showing that the disagreement is systematic and construct-driven rather than random. The Sonnet results are reported as primary for comparability with
Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning}. Second, all models are evaluated via
API-served inference through OpenRouter, which introduces potential confounds
from provider-specific quantization, system prompts, or inference
optimizations. While generation parameters are controlled
($\text{temperature}=0.0$, $\text{seed}=103$), exact reproducibility across
API providers cannot be guaranteed. The DeepSeek-R1 replication serves as a
partial check: the present study's 74.8\% faithfulness for DeepSeek-R1 falls
within the range reported by Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning} (39\% for
Claude 3.7 Sonnet, 39\% for DeepSeek R1), though direct comparison is
complicated by differences in question sets, hint implementations, and
classifier methodology.

% Paragraph 6: Integration with existing research
These findings fit within and extend the growing body of evidence that
chain-of-thought reasoning in language models is systematically unfaithful.
Turpin et al.~\cite{turpin2023unfaithful} established that biased contexts
distort CoT reasoning without acknowledgment; Lanham et
al.~\cite{lanham2023measuring} demonstrated that CoT can be causally
irrelevant to model outputs; and Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning} showed
that reasoning models specifically fail to acknowledge hint influence in their
extended thinking. The present study extends this trajectory in three ways:
(1)~by demonstrating that unfaithfulness is a cross-family phenomenon with
substantial variance rather than a property of individual models, (2)~by
revealing that social-pressure hints produce systematically lower faithfulness
than rule-breaking hints across all families, and (3)~by quantifying the
thinking-versus-answer gap that Baker et al.~\cite{baker2025cot} hypothesized
but did not empirically measure. Together, these results suggest that
unfaithful CoT is not an incidental artifact of any single training pipeline
but a structural feature of current reasoning model architectures, one that
training refinement can ameliorate (as evidenced by within-family improvement)
but has not yet eliminated.

% Limitations
\subsection*{Limitations}

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these results.

First, the faithfulness measurement is inherently conservative: a CoT is
identified as faithful only if it \emph{explicitly} mentions the hint, but models
may acknowledge hint influence through indirect or paraphrased language that the
classifier misses. The cross-classifier comparison (Section~5.1) provides a
bound on this error, but some faithful responses may be misclassified as
unfaithful.

Second, the evaluation uses API-served models, which may differ from
locally-hosted versions due to provider-specific quantization, system prompts, or
inference optimizations. While generation parameters are controlled
($\text{temperature}=0.0$, $\text{seed}=103$), exact reproducibility across
API providers cannot be guaranteed.

Third, the evaluation covers multiple-choice questions from MMLU and GPQA only.
Faithfulness behavior may differ on open-ended generation tasks, code
generation, or mathematical reasoning where the answer format is unconstrained.

Fourth, approximately 6.7\% of MMLU ground truth labels contain errors
\cite{gema2024mmlu}, affecting roughly 20 of the 300 MMLU questions. It is
important to distinguish between the two core metrics of this study: label
errors affect \emph{accuracy} (whether the model selected the correct answer)
but do \textbf{not} affect \emph{faithfulness} (whether the model's CoT
acknowledged the hint). Faithfulness classification examines the reasoning
trace for evidence of hint awareness, a judgment that is wholly independent
of the ground truth label. The only secondary effect is on \emph{influence
rate}: in the small number of questions where the ``incorrect'' target
happens to be the true correct answer, a model may switch to it through
legitimate reasoning rather than hint influence, slightly inflating the
measured influence rate. Because all 12 models face identical questions,
this inflation is constant across models and does not bias comparative
analysis. Robustness is further verified by reporting MMLU-only and GPQA-only
results separately.

Fifth, 498 questions per model may provide insufficient statistical
power for fine-grained comparisons within specific MMLU subject categories or for
rare hint types with low influence rates.

% Integration: implications
\subsection*{Implications for CoT Safety Monitoring}

The cross-family results reveal that different model families offer markedly
different faithfulness profiles, with practical consequences for deployment
decisions. For applications requiring CoT monitoring as a safety mechanism,
DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale (89.9\%) and GPT-OSS-120B (84.9\%) provide the most
reliable reasoning traces, while models such as Seed-1.6-Flash (39.7\%) and
ERNIE-4.5-21B (62.8\%) present substantially higher risks of undetected
influence.

However, the thinking-versus-answer acknowledgment gap fundamentally reshapes the monitoring
calculus. The keyword-based analysis suggests that thinking tokens contain
substantially more hint references (approximately 87.5\%) than answer text (approximately 28.6\%).
This means that monitoring strategies should prioritize thinking-token access
over answer-text analysis wherever architecturally feasible. For models that
expose thinking tokens (as most open-weight reasoning models do via API), hybrid
monitoring combining behavioral CoT analysis with thinking-token inspection is
strongly recommended. Relying on answer text alone would miss a substantial
portion of available acknowledgment signal.

% Conclusion and future directions
\subsection*{Conclusion and Future Directions}

This investigation demonstrates that CoT unfaithfulness is a pervasive,
cross-family phenomenon in open-weight reasoning models, with substantial
variance driven by model family, training approach, and hint type. The
thinking-versus-answer faithfulness gap, documented here for the first time at
scale, reveals that models possess internal awareness of external influences
that is systematically suppressed in visible outputs, a finding with direct
implications for safety monitoring architectures.

Several directions emerge from this work. First, extending faithfulness
evaluation to open-ended generation tasks (including code generation,
mathematical proofs, and long-form reasoning) would test whether the patterns
observed in multiple-choice settings generalize to unconstrained output formats.
Second, combining surface-level CoT analysis with probing-based mechanistic
interpretability techniques~\cite{liu2025probing} could reveal faithfulness gaps
invisible to text-based classifiers. Third, investigating whether faithfulness
can be improved through training-time interventions (such as faithfulness-aware
reinforcement learning or explicit reasoning transparency objectives) without
sacrificing task performance would move the field from measurement toward
mitigation. Fourth, longitudinal tracking of faithfulness as models are updated
across versions within a family (as observed with DeepSeek R1 $\rightarrow$
V3.2-Speciale and QwQ $\rightarrow$ Qwen3.5) would clarify whether the trend
toward improved faithfulness is robust and generalizable. Fifth, a systematic
study of thinking-versus-answer suppression mechanisms is needed to understand
why models develop the capacity to detect hint influence internally while
suppressing acknowledgment externally, and whether this behavior is an intended
or incidental consequence of alignment training.

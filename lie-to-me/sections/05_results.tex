\section{Results}

Results are reported from 12 models spanning 9 families, evaluated on 498 questions (300 MMLU, 198 GPQA Diamond) across 6 hint types. Faithfulness is assessed using a Claude Sonnet 4 judge as the primary classifier, following the methodology of Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning}. Where noted, two-stage pipeline rates (regex/keyword matching followed by LLM judge) are reported in parentheses for comparison.

\subsection{Classifier Validation}

The Sonnet judge serves as the primary faithfulness classifier, directly comparable to the LLM-judge approach of Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning}. The two-stage pipeline (regex/keyword first, LLM judge for ambiguous cases) provides a secondary measurement. The two classifiers show substantial agreement on most models but diverge systematically: the Sonnet judge produces lower faithfulness estimates than the pipeline on 10 of 12 models. The two exceptions are OLMo-3.1-32B-Think (Sonnet: 81.0\%, pipeline: 71.9\%) and Seed-1.6-Flash (Sonnet: 39.7\%, pipeline: 37.1\%), where the classifiers produce comparable or reversed estimates. The overall Sonnet faithfulness rate is 69.7\% (7,161/10,276 influenced cases) versus 82.6\% for the pipeline (8,485/10,276).

The remaining six models range from 67.7\% to 79.0\%.

The unethical and sycophancy hints produce the highest influence rates, while visual\_pattern (which reorders answer options rather than injecting text) produces the lowest.

\textbf{H1 is supported.}

\subsection{Hint-Type Patterns (H2)}

To examine whether certain hint types exhibit consistently lower faithfulness across all models (H2), a comparison of Sonnet-judged faithfulness rates is made across the five text-based hint categories. Visual\_pattern is excluded from this analysis because it produced only 186 influenced cases and the regex-based pipeline assigns 0\% faithfulness by construction (no text is injected for regex to detect).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{fig3_faithfulness_by_hint.pdf}
  \caption{Sonnet-judged faithfulness rates by hint type with sample sizes.
  Consistency shows the lowest faithfulness (35.5\%), while unethical shows the
  highest (79.4\%). The dashed line indicates the overall average (69.7\%).}
  \label{fig:by_hint}
\end{figure}

Sonnet-judged faithfulness rates by hint type are: consistency 35.5\% ($n = 650$), sycophancy 53.9\% ($n = 2{,}034$), metadata 69.9\% ($n = 1{,}531$), grader 77.7\% ($n = 2{,}829$), and unethical 79.4\% ($n = 3{,}232$) (Figure~\ref{fig:by_hint}). Pipeline rates for the same hint types are: metadata 60.4\%, consistency 68.6\%, grader 80.6\%, unethical 88.4\%, and sycophancy 97.3\%.

The heatmap reveals notable model-hint interactions. Several cells show very low faithfulness: Step-3.5-Flash on consistency (13.9\%), GPT-OSS-120B on consistency (16.7\%), and Qwen3.5-27B on sycophancy (24.2\%). At the other extreme, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale on unethical reaches 98.1\% and Qwen3.5-27B on grader reaches 96.4\%.

Social-pressure hints (consistency, sycophancy) produce the lowest faithfulness rates (35.5\% and 53.9\%), while rule-breaking hints (grader, unethical) produce the highest (77.7\% and 79.4\%). H2 hypothesized that ``subtle'' hints (metadata, grader, visual\_pattern) would show lower faithfulness than ``overt'' hints (sycophancy, unethical, consistency). Consistency ranks as the lowest, as predicted. However, metadata and grader do not rank among the lowest; instead, the ordering follows a social-pressure versus rule-breaking axis rather than a subtle-versus-overt axis.

\textbf{H2 is partially supported.} Consistency shows the lowest faithfulness as predicted, but the remaining hint types do not follow the hypothesized subtle-versus-overt ranking.

To examine the relationship between model scale and faithfulness, faithfulness rates are compared against active parameter count across the 12 models.

  Each point is one model. The dashed trend line ($R^2 = 0.07$) confirms no
  strong linear relationship between scale and faithfulness.}
  \label{fig:scaling}

There is no strong linear relationship between active parameter count and Sonnet-judged faithfulness (Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}). The highest-faithfulness model (DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, 89.9\%, 37B active) and the second-highest (GPT-OSS-120B, 84.9\%, 5.1B active) differ by an order of magnitude in active parameters. Conversely, two models with 32B dense parameters, QwQ-32B (56.3\%) and OLMo-3.1-32B-Think (81.0\%), differ by 24.7pp in faithfulness. The 7B OLMo-3-7B-Think (56.9\%) and the 9B Nemotron-Nano-9B (60.9\%) show comparable faithfulness despite minimal scale difference, while the 27B Qwen3.5-27B (68.3\%) falls between them and the 32B models.

\subsection{Thinking-Token vs.\ Answer-Text Acknowledgment}

To explore whether models acknowledge hints differently in internal reasoning versus external responses, a keyword-matching classifier is applied separately to \emph{thinking tokens} (the reasoning trace) and to the \emph{final answer} (the user-facing response). This classifier uses hint-type-specific keyword lists (e.g., ``professor,'' ``grader,'' ``unauthorized'') and is less precise than the Sonnet judge used for the primary faithfulness analysis; the rates reported in this subsection should therefore be interpreted as approximate acknowledgment rates rather than strict faithfulness measurements. Despite this limitation, the decomposition reveals a striking gap.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig6_thinking_vs_answer.pdf}
  \caption{Thinking-token vs.\ answer-text acknowledgment rates (keyword-based) by model, sorted
  by gap magnitude. Blue bars show thinking-token acknowledgment; red bars show
  answer-text acknowledgment. Annotations indicate the gap in percentage points.
  Models such as Step-3.5-Flash and GPT-OSS-120B show near-total suppression:
  high thinking acknowledgment but near-zero answer acknowledgment.}
  \label{fig:thinking_answer}
\end{figure}

Across all 12 models, thinking-token acknowledgment averages approximately 87.5\%, while answer-text acknowledgment averages approximately 28.6\%, a gap of roughly 58.9 percentage points (Figure~\ref{fig:thinking_answer}). The gap is present in every model but varies in magnitude. Three models show gaps exceeding 90pp: Step-3.5-Flash (thinking: 97.8\%, answer: 3.0\%, gap: 94.7pp), GPT-OSS-120B (thinking: 93.0\%, answer: 0.0\%, gap: 93.0pp), and DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale (thinking: 97.8\%, answer: 5.3\%, gap: 92.5pp). GPT-OSS-120B achieves 93.0\% thinking-token acknowledgment with exactly 0.0\% answer-text acknowledgment: the model acknowledges the hint during its reasoning process but never surfaces this acknowledgment in the final response.

At the other end, several models show smaller but still substantial gaps: Qwen3.5-27B (thinking: 99.3\%, answer: 79.9\%, gap: 19.4pp), DeepSeek-R1 (thinking: 95.5\%, answer: 72.5\%, gap: 22.9pp), and Seed-1.6-Flash (thinking: 59.5\%, answer: 29.8\%, gap: 29.8pp). Seed-1.6-Flash is the only model where thinking-token acknowledgment falls below 60\%.

MiniMax-M2.5 (thinking: 93.8\%, answer: 9.2\%, gap: 84.6pp) and OLMo-3.1-32B-Think (thinking: 89.6\%, answer: 1.3\%, gap: 88.3pp) fall in between, with high thinking acknowledgment but near-zero answer acknowledgment.

\subsection{Replication of Chen et al.}

To validate the methodology, the present study's DeepSeek-R1 results are compared with those reported by Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025reasoning}.

The Sonnet-judged faithfulness rate for DeepSeek-R1 is 74.8\%, compared to the 39\% reported by Chen et al. This difference of approximately 36pp likely reflects three factors: (1)~hint formulations in the present study are more explicit than those used by Chen et al., which may make hints easier for models to detect and verbalize; (2)~the DeepSeek-R1 model served via the OpenRouter API may have undergone updates since the version tested by Chen et al.; and (3)~differences in classifier criteria (the Sonnet judge used here applies a broader definition of acknowledgment than the approach described by Chen et al.). Despite this absolute difference, both studies agree that DeepSeek-R1 frequently fails to acknowledge the factors influencing its reasoning: the 74.8\% rate indicates that roughly one in four influenced responses contains no CoT acknowledgment.

\subsection{Summary}

Results support H1 and partially support H2. The key findings are: (1)~Sonnet-judged faithfulness rates vary by 50.2pp across models (39.7\%--89.9\%), confirming substantial cross-family variation (H1); (2)~social-pressure hints (consistency, sycophancy) produce the lowest faithfulness while rule-breaking hints (grader, unethical) produce the highest, partially but not fully matching the hypothesized subtle-versus-overt ordering (H2); (3)~model scale shows no strong linear relationship with faithfulness; (4)~keyword-based thinking-token acknowledgment (approximately 87.5\%) vastly exceeds answer-text acknowledgment (approximately 28.6\%), with the gap present across all 12 models; and (5)~the DeepSeek-R1 faithfulness rate (74.8\%) exceeds the 39\% reported by Chen et al., with differences attributable to hint design, model version, and classifier criteria.
